## Parr Show-vs-Tell Experiment

**Source basis:** art_01M6Q8W5S3ASWTWKS200QHGFVD revision 1

## Runner checklist
- [ ] Assign reader to exactly one version (A or B). Do not reveal there is another version.
- [ ] Read the version aloud OR hand the printed sheet. Do not add commentary.
- [ ] Ask the three questions below in order. Record verbatim; do not prompt with trait words.
- [ ] Log version assignment, question order and any deviation from protocol in NOTES column.

---

## Three prompts (verbatim — do not paraphrase)
1. "What can you tell me about Mira?"
2. "What did she do?"
3. "How confident are you about that?"

---

## Word-type taxonomy
Classify each noun, verb and adjective in the reader's Q1 response:

| Code | Type | Examples |
|------|------|----------|
| L | Evaluative label | kind, helpful, generous, stupid |
| B | Behavioural description | watched, said, waited, moved away |
| I | Inferential claim | "she probably wanted", "she seemed to" |
| U | Unclassifiable | filler, pronouns, articles |

Record one code per word. Tally L and B counts per reader.

---

## Discriminating outcome
- **If B > L on average in version B and L >= B in version A:** consistent with Parr's claim.
- **If both versions produce similar L/B ratios:** narrows where the technique matters.
- **If version A produces more B responses than B:** revision needed — something in A is already action-signalling.

Log all three outcomes. The null result is the most informative.

---

## Separation: observation vs taste
Record visibility or comprehension problems in NOTES before asking about preference.
Do not ask "did you like it?" until after Q3 is logged.
