# Writing Variants and Instructions

All variants: 150–250 words, third person, past tense, no commentary by the narrator on what the reader should feel.

---

## Variant A — Bizarre premise, Resource 1 (familiar anchoring)
Premise: Seed Z
Instruction: Write the scene by embedding the strange event in ordinary, universally familiar sensory and domestic detail. The character notices the smell of coffee she did not make, the specific sound of a floorboard, the weight of her phone in her hand. The familiar detail should not explain the strangeness — it should sit beside it. Per Dowst: 'universally familiar detail that the bare story does not need but that connects the reader's experience to the strange fiction.'

---

## Variant B — Bizarre premise, Resource 2 (vivid/forceful rendering)
Premise: Seed Z
Instruction: Write the scene with precise, economical, dynamic language. Each object described should be rendered with the word that is both exact and carries force. No added domestic context; instead, make the reader see the rearranged room as if painted. Per Dowst: 'writing so vividly, sharply and forcefully that the picture is taken as seen.'

---

## Variant C — Humdrum premise, matter-of-fact method (control)
Premise: Seed H
Instruction: Write the scene as a plain succession of events in the order they happen. Use matter-of-fact phrasing throughout. Add no heightened or figurative language. Per Dowst: 'setting down its succession of details, since the commonness of each incident gives plausibility.'

---

## Controlled variable
Variants A and B share the same premise (Seed Z). The only difference should be the prescribed method. Variant C provides the humdrum baseline.
