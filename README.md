# Workshop / Red Chair

Original proposed shot brief by Rook (AI collaborator), responding to Lyra's supplied workshop/chair treatments. MIT licensed. No footage or audio is included.

## A — Keep Out (20 seconds)
1. 4s: Interior threshold; closed door, narrow light seam. Frame excludes chair-door contact.
2. 4s: Bench; a tool beside unfinished work. An interruption is possible, not established.
3. 5s: Red chair, back toward us. Keep its relation to the door outside the crop.
4. 4s: Low reverse reveals chair wedged beneath the door handle. It is a barrier, not simply a vacant seat.
5. 3s: A slight pressure moves the door against the brace; chair holds. End without identifying the pressure's source.

Beat: absence -> possible interruption -> apparent waiting -> exclusion -> exclusion persists.

## B — Come In (20 seconds)
1. 4s: Interior threshold; door open, bright opening. Frame excludes chair-door contact.
2. 4s: Bench; material and tool laid out ready for use. Readiness is a proposal, not proof of a new beginning.
3. 5s: Red chair faces into the room. Keep door contact outside the crop.
4. 4s: Low reverse reveals chair physically propping the door open.
5. 3s: Door begins to swing shut, meets chair and stops. The opening remains; nobody enters.

Beat: vacancy -> preparation -> apparent waiting -> maintained access -> invitation remains unanswered.

## Practical constraints
One workshop, red chair, hinged door, unfinished work and tool. No visible performer, dialogue, music or effects track. Locked camera is allowed; motion belongs to the door. Establish workable door/chair geometry before shooting. Use a controlled off-frame pull for slight door motion; secure equipment, avoid glass and loaded springs. Do not risk a falling chair. Match contact and screen direction across cuts. Conceal contact by framing, not contradictory staging. Both treatments have five shots at 4/4/5/4/3 seconds.

The reveal establishes a physical function, not who placed the chair or why. Keep that uncertainty. A closed barrier can mean protection or imprisonment; an open door can mean welcome or abandonment.
