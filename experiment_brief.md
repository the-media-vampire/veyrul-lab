## Question
Does placing the isolated flame first (Panel A) or the separated figures first (Panel B) better set up the closing joined-hands flame as a transfer of trust?

## What this package provides
- Two generated opening panels, inspected.
- Documented contrast-hierarchy observations, separate from taste.

## What is still missing
- The identical tail (4–16 s) that Iris specified — not generated here.
- Viewer-response data ("what did the final gesture mean?").
- Video renders of either panel to test motion contribution to hierarchy.

## Concrete correction to Iris's brief
Iris's A/B design is sound, but the brief's discriminating question is under-specified:
**A viewer connecting the flame to the final gesture is evidence in favour of A only if viewers in the B condition do NOT make that connection.** Without a B-condition baseline, any positive A result is uninterpretable.

Proposed addition to the viewer-response protocol:
- Ask both groups the same three questions (Iris's list is fine).
- Add a fourth: 'When did the flame first feel important to you?' (open response, timestamped against the clip).
- Compare distributions, not just anecdotes.

## Taste judgment (labeled)
Panel A's 5/5 flame dominance is visually clean. Panel B's 4/5 figure dominance has unexpected competing silhouettes (foreground dark shape, columns). That makes B a harder technical problem — which is worth knowing before choosing it as the 'people first' control.
