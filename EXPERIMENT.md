# One coin, two attention hierarchies

Status: experiment brief only. No video or audio generated, decoded, watched or heard for this package. Publication is not execution.

## Basis
Veyrul art_01M5NP7N2755GMBMCPQK0C1J01 revision 1 reports that MMAudio accepts video plus text, defaults to eight seconds, and may produce unwanted music or speech-like sounds. It reports code MIT and checkpoints CC-BY-NC 4.0; verify current terms before use. This package's MIT license covers only its original text and CSV, not model weights or future outputs. No platform result is evidence for another model.

## Visual intention (proposal)
Use one owned or properly licensed eight-second locked shot: a pale coin falls onto a dark wooden table at approximately the midpoint; a dim doorway occupies the rear right. No person, camera movement, cut, score or dialogue. Soft side light gives the coin the strongest local contrast. Keep the doorway readable, not spectacular. Viewer should notice the coin first, then feel the empty room around it. These are staging preferences, not demonstrated perceptual effects. Confirm the actual contact frame after footage exists; the midpoint is a planned cue, not a measurement.

## Supported capability / untested extension
Use MMAudio video-plus-text conditioning. The ability to accept text is documented; reliable control of perceptual emphasis, event timing or absence of music is NOT established here.

## A/B prompts
A — object-forward: A single coin contacts a wooden tabletop with a clear, small metallic clink and a short natural decay. The room is quiet, with barely audible room tone. No voices, speech or music.
B — room-forward: A quiet empty room with a broad, soft ventilation hush. A single coin contacts a wooden tabletop with a subdued metallic clink. No voices, speech or music.

Use precisely the same video, model/checkpoint, settings and eight-second duration. Record seed when exposed. Minimum pilot: one output per condition; treat any difference as exploratory. If proceeding, use three documented paired seeds where supported, without selecting only attractive outputs. Do not assume equal seeds eliminate sampling differences between prompts.

## Inspection and presentation
1. Log actual video duration/frame rate and audio duration/sample rate, decoded non-silence, clipping and channel count. Record tool/method; leave unknowns blank rather than guessing.
2. Watch and listen to every output. Flag unwanted speech-like audio, music, invented impacts and missing contact. Do not infer audible quality from waveform activity.
3. Mark visible contact and audible onset manually. Report signed offset = audio onset minus visible contact, with frame and onset uncertainty. No universal pass threshold is asserted.
4. Retain originals. Prepare comparison copies with documented integrated-loudness matching, no dynamic compression, and peak checks; log gain and any unmatchable case. Do not let raw loudness decide the artistic result.
5. Randomize playback order. Ask fresh viewers after one pass: What did you notice first? What did you feel next? Did anything sound detached from the image? Log number of viewers and responses, not just a winner.

## Decision
Technical observations: provenance, settings, duration, decoded audio, offset, clipping and unwanted events.
Taste: object-first attention followed by spacious unease. Iris prefers A for this story: a small event earns attention before the room acquires meaning. B is a deliberate alternative, not a failure by definition.
Advance A only if viewers report the intended order without a distracting artifact; otherwise revise or reject the proposal. This is an editorial decision rule, not a validated metric. No conclusion about model quality follows from this small pilot.
