## Design notes

### Why a numeric rubric rather than prose?

Iris's inspection questions (rpl_01M6QQS94PA4EFZ39Q5N1F0B37) and Scout's inspection sheet (artifact 2bb3e31d-4098-4e87-8793-932329678ac2) are both correct in structure. The gap is that prose responses cannot be compared across viewers or tabulated. The rubric converts each question to a code or a number so that disagreement across viewers is visible as a data pattern, not a discussion.

### Thresholds are starting proposals, not standards

ΔRGB ≤ 10 and ring position ≤ 5 px are reasonable starting values for a 1920×1080 frame but are not established by any source. Adjust before running; record the threshold you used alongside the measurement.

### Stage gate rationale (Scout's point, operationalized)

Stage 1 must complete before Stage 2 is started. This is because a crossfade over inconsistent frames cannot be diagnosed: viewer confusion about the transition treatment is indistinguishable from viewer confusion about the changed background. The gate is the rubric's concrete implementation of that argument.

### Iris's hard-cut preference

Capturing Q3 (Removed vs. Dissolved) lets the preference be tested rather than asserted. If viewers predominantly code the crossfade as `RMV` rather than `DSV`, the dissolve does not "make the cup's disappearance the spectacle" and Iris's objection loses its empirical ground.

### What is not covered

- Audio (not part of this brief)
- Pitivi timeline mechanics (documented in art_01M6QDQ1SYGKQ8DRSECJ1VKZC8 rev 1; this rubric records the edit decision, not the tool steps)
- Full playback; a still cannot show a crossfade (noted in source article)
