# Stimulus specimens — Dowst humdrum vs. bizarre trial

All three passages share the same narrator voice (first-person past tense), the same ~110-word target, and the same vocabulary register (reading level ~Grade 8). Content is fictional.

Depends on: `4294ad45-e562-405b-92c3-a162cf40b241` (design brief, commit b0b5b0f7abb)

---

## Variant A — Bizarre premise / Resource 1 (familiar anchoring)

> Every night for a week the furniture in my sitting room had moved.
> Not fallen, not shifted — moved, with purpose. The armchair, the one
> my mother recovered in blue corduroy the summer I was twelve, now
> stood squarely under the window. The rug still held the warm dent
> where the chair had spent ten years. I pressed my foot into that dent
> the way you press a bruise, to confirm it. The table lamp, its shade
> slightly crooked since I last bumped it on a Tuesday in March, was
> against the east wall. Each object was exactly where a tidy person
> might have put it, which was what made it frightening.

*Method note (not shown to readers): strange events are narrated through accumulated familiar detail — a named fabric, a remembered dent, a dated lamp nudge — anchoring the reader in ordinary life before the strangeness can fully land.*

## Variant B — Bizarre premise / Resource 2 (vivid/forceful rendering)

> The chair was under the window. It had not been there the night before.
> I looked at it. I circled it. The light from the window cut across the
> seat at the exact angle it would have cut if that chair had always stood
> there, as if the room itself had decided this was correct and had made
> it so. The rug held a pale rectangle where the chair had been; the paint
> behind where the chair now stood was untouched, pale as a closed eye.
> I did not look away. I wanted to look away. The chair did not move.
> I could have stated facts. The facts did not add up to what I saw.

*Method note: rendered with declarative exactness and controlled repetition; no familiar biographical detail — the strangeness is conveyed by accumulating precise description until the picture holds itself.*

## Variant C — Humdrum premise / matter-of-fact succession (control)

> On Saturday I decided to rearrange the sitting room. I moved the
> armchair under the window to get better light for reading. The rug
> did not reach the new position, so I centered it differently and
> made a note to find a larger one. I slid the table lamp to the east
> wall, which shortened the cord; a small cable clip from the hardware
> drawer solved that. By midday the room looked cleaner than it had.
> I swept up the dust from behind the displaced furniture, wiped the
> skirting boards and left the window open to air the room. By evening
> I had forgotten it had ever been arranged any other way.

*Method note: plain succession of practical steps, matter-of-fact phrasing, no expressive highlighting — the chapter's prescription for the humdrum story.*
